Why BCT Is Not a Dominant Confound

A lesson in marginal tests, shared variance, and why your classifier can "predict" something that isn't really there.

Prerequisites: None — this lesson is the entry point.

The paradox

You've just run the diagnostic protocol and seen two results that seem to contradict each other:

MethodFinding
Classifier negative controlBCT can be predicted with F1 = 0.294–0.734 — sometimes rivaling Tissue F1
PERMANOVA (marginal)BCT's unique R² = 0.004–0.021 — essentially zero

How can a classifier predict BCT if PERMANOVA says BCT explains almost nothing?

This is the central puzzle. Let's build intuition for how both can be true.

Shared variance: the umbrella-sales analogy

Suppose you collect daily data on two variables:

VariableValue
Umbrella sales20/day on rainy days, 2/day on sunny days
Ice cream sales10/day on rainy days, 200/day on sunny days
Weather (actual)Hidden — you don't measure it directly
Thought experiment: Can you predict umbrella sales from ice cream sales? Yes — they're correlated through weather. On low-ice-cream days, umbrella sales are higher. But ice cream doesn't cause umbrella sales.

Now replace the variables:

AnalogyOur data
Umbrella salesBCT — correlates with Tissue
Ice cream salesTissue — the biological variable of interest
Hidden weatherThe biological mechanism that drives both methylation patterns and tissue identity

A classifier can predict BCT because BCT and Tissue are correlated — not because BCT has independent signal in the features.

Why are BCT and Tissue correlated?

Look at the contingency table from our actual data:

Key patterns:

  • ACD → used only for colon. If you know a sample is ACD, it must be colon.
  • Streck → used only for Sowalsky prostate. If you know Streck, it must be prostate.
  • Citrate → used for liver and pancreas. If you know Citrate, it's almost certainly liver or pancreas.
  • EDTA → used for everything else. The "default" tube.

When a classifier learns "what pattern of methylation looks like colon," it simultaneously learns "what pattern of methylation looks like ACD" — because every colon sample happens to be either ACD or EDTA, and every ACD sample happens to be colon.

How PERMANOVA strips away shared variance

Sequential (Type I) — first-in wins

If you ask PERMANOVA: "How much variance does BCT explain when we let it enter the model first?"

ModalityBCT enters first (R²)BCT enters last (R²)
FEM40.1550.018
Fragment length0.0730.004
Probe methylation0.0320.005

When BCT enters first, it claims all the variance it shares with Tissue. The R² looks meaningful.

Marginal (Type III) — each term gets only its unique contribution

The marginal test asks a stricter question: "After Tissue, Source, and Sex are already accounted for, does BCT add anything new?"

This is the "last-in" column above. For FEM4, BCT's unique R² drops from 0.155 to 0.018. The difference (0.155 − 0.018 = 0.137) is the variance that BCT shares with Tissue.

In the umbrella-ice-cream analogy: the marginal test is like asking "does ice cream sales tell us anything about umbrella sales beyond what we already know from weather data?" The answer is no — the apparent relationship was entirely through the hidden weather variable.

This is the core insight. The vast majority of what looks like "BCT signal" is actually Tissue signal that BCT happens to correlate with.

But why does the classifier find BCT?

A classifier doesn't distinguish between unique and shared variance. It asks: "Is there any pattern in these features that lets me predict BCT?"

Because BCT and Tissue are correlated, the answer is "yes." The classifier finds the Tissue pattern and uses it to predict BCT as a side effect. This is exactly the umbrella-sales-from-ice-cream phenomenon.

If you trained a classifier to predict BCT after regressing out Tissue (i.e., on the residuals), its F1 would collapse to chance. We didn't do that step — but the PERMANOVA marginal test is that step for distance-based variance.

The smoking gun: EDTA subset

The strongest evidence comes from the EDTA-only subset (96 samples where BCT is held constant). When BCT cannot vary:

The tissue signal doesn't collapse — it strengthens. When BCT noise is removed, the biological signal becomes clearer. BCT-F1 drops to 0.000 (no BCT variation to predict). This is the definitive proof that BCT was a passenger, not a driver.

Why the verdict is "Borderline" not "Weak"

The adjudication uses a two-criteria threshold:

CriterionValueThreshold
PERMANOVA marginal R²0.004–0.021≥ 0.10 → Material/Dominant
Classifier F1 (BCT)0.294–0.734≥ 0.8 × Tissue F1 → suspect

BCT fails the first criterion (R² way below 0.10) but passes the second (F1 often ≥ 0.8 × Tissue F1). The "Borderline" verdict means: the classifier signal is suspicious and needs investigation — which we did via entry-order sensitivity and EDTA subset. The investigation proves the signal is aliasing, not real.

What would a "Dominant" confound look like?

For BCT to be dominant, we'd need both:

  1. R² ≥ 0.10 in the marginal test (BCT explains unique variance after Tissue)
  2. Classifier F1 ≥ 0.8 × Tissue F1 (BCT is learnable)

Neither is true for unique variance. The classifier finding alone is not enough — that's why the protocol uses converging evidence from four different methods.

Why this matters for the project

  • Batch correction (ComBat, harmony) is unnecessary — it would remove biological signal aliased with BCT while fixing nothing real.
  • Source-stratified CV is the real need — Source has consistent unique R² of 0.014–0.052.
  • Single-source tissues (colon, pancreas) are usable for within-batch comparisons but cannot support cross-source generalization.

Key takeaways

  1. Correlation ≠ causation. A classifier finding pattern in BCT does not mean BCT is an independent signal.
  2. Marginal (Type III) tests strip shared variance. They answer "what does this variable add that nothing else already explains?"
  3. Entry order reveals shared variance. The gap between BCT-first and last-in R² measures how much variance BCT shares with Tissue.
  4. EDTA subset is the definitive experiment. When BCT can't vary and tissue signal persists, BCT was never the confound you feared.
  5. Converging evidence is essential. No single method (not PCA, not PERMANOVA, not a classifier) is sufficient — you need the full matrix of 4 methods × 6 modalities.

Check your understanding:

A colleague says: "But the classifier can predict BCT with F1=0.73 — that means BCT is a real signal in the features. We need batch correction."

How do you respond? (Think before reading on.)

The classifier finding alone is not evidence of unique BCT signal. The PERMANOVA marginal test shows BCT adds only R²=0.018 after accounting for Tissue — and the EDTA subset proves it: when BCT is held constant, the tissue signal persists (R²=0.178 vs 0.150). The classifier found BCT because BCT and Tissue are correlated (Cramer's V structure: ACD→colon, Streck→prostate). Batch correction would remove biological signal that happens to correlate with BCT, not fix a real confound.
